SD-DPC: Sparse Dictionary Differentiable Predictive Control

Ali Reza Daneshvar Garmroodi, Jan Drgoňa
Concordia University  ·  Johns Hopkins University
Preprint, 2026
Paper PDF Code

Abstract

We present sparse dictionary differentiable predictive control (SD-DPC), a framework for learning sparse, interpretable feedback policies for nonlinear systems from data. A prediction model is first identified by rollout-based sparse identification of nonlinear dynamics (SINDy), building on gradient-based and multistep formulations.

The policy is then parameterized as a sparse combination of dictionary functions and trained by differentiating a constrained finite-horizon predictive-control objective through this model, so that its terms are selected by closed-loop performance rather than by imitating a previously trained controller. The result is an explicit feedback law with only a handful of terms.

Across three benchmark control problems, SD-DPC satisfies the constraints in all test scenarios, outperforms a policy distilled onto the same terms by up to an order of magnitude, and requires orders of magnitude less memory and online computation than an optimization benchmark, while admitting explicit sensitivity bounds.

Methodology

SD-DPC proceeds in two stages. Both are instances of the same sequentially thresholded gradient descent, applied to different losses: coefficients are updated by a first-order method with a subgradient for the \(\ell_1\) term, pruned entrywise every \(n_p\) epochs, and finally refitted on the retained set without the penalty to remove its shrinkage bias.

Sparse identification from rollout error

The dynamics are written as \(\dot x \approx \Theta(x,u)\,\Xi\) over a library of candidate functions, and the coefficients are fit from multistep rollout error rather than from state derivatives, so numerical differentiation never enters the objective:

$$\min_{\Xi}\ \frac{1}{m_{\mathrm{id}}}\sum_{j}\Big(Q_1\|x_1^j-\hat x_1^j\|_2^2 +Q_N\!\!\sum_{k=2}^{N_{\mathrm{id}}}\!\|x_k^j-\hat x_k^j\|_2^2\Big)+\lambda\|\Xi\|_1 ,$$ $$x_{k+1}^j=\mathrm{ODESolve}\big(\hat f,\ x_k^j,\ \hat u_k^j,\ \Delta t\big).$$

With a one-step horizon and an explicit Euler step this reduces to the \(\ell_1\)-regularized SINDy regression with forward-difference derivatives; the multistep terms penalize the error accumulated along the rollout.

Sparse dictionary policy

The feedback law uses a second library over the policy input \(z_t=[x_t\ \psi_t]\) and is projected onto the admissible input set, so input constraints hold by construction and each channel retains its own subset of terms:

$$u_t=\pi_{\Xi_\pi}(z_t)=\Pi_{\mathcal U}\big(\Theta_\pi(z_t)\,\Xi_\pi\big).$$

The forward pass evaluates this projection exactly, while the backward pass uses a banded, leaky surrogate derivative. A coefficient feeding a saturated channel therefore still receives gradient information, without being credited with a sensitivity the plant does not have.

Policy learning

The coefficients are obtained by differentiating a constrained predictive-control objective through closed-loop rollouts of the identified model, over sampled initial conditions and reference realizations:

$$\min_{\Xi_{\pi}}\ \frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\Big(\sum_{t=1}^{N} \ell_{\mathrm{DPC}}(x_t,u_{t-1},\psi_t)+\ell_N(x_N,\psi_N)\Big)+\lambda_\pi\|\Xi_\pi\|_1 ,$$ $$x_{t+1}=F_{\Xi}(x_t,u_t),\qquad u_t=\pi_{\Xi_\pi}(z_t).$$

The stage loss collects the tracking objective, control regularization, and differentiable penalties on state-constraint violation. Two properties follow from the pruning mechanism. A pruned coefficient is never retained again, so the retained set changes at most \(pn\) times and the thresholding loop terminates within \(n_pM(pn+1)\) epochs for any loss. And because the policy is a finite sum of known functions, its Lipschitz constant \(L_\pi\) follows directly from the retained coefficients and the library slopes, which bounds the closed-loop deviation caused by pruning or by model mismatch.

Case studies

Three systems are considered. In each case the policy is trained on the identified model and then evaluated on the true plant, so the reported results include model mismatch.

Two-tank system. A pump feeds an upper tank that drains by gravity into a lower one. The states are the two liquid levels and the inputs are the pump command and the valve split,

$$\dot x_1=c_1(1-u_2)u_1-c_2\sqrt{x_1},\qquad \dot x_2=c_1u_2u_1+c_2\sqrt{x_1}-c_2\sqrt{x_2}.$$

The task is to track time-varying level references with levels in \([0,1]\) and inputs in \([0,1]^2\), with the level bounds entering the loss as hinge penalties.

Forced Van der Pol oscillator. The uncontrolled trajectories converge to an attracting limit cycle surrounding an unstable origin,

$$\dot x_1=x_2,\qquad \dot x_2=\mu(1-x_1^2)x_2-x_1+u,\qquad \mu=1.$$

The task is to regulate the state to the origin under \(|u|\le5\) and \(|x_i|\le5\), with initial conditions sampled both on and off the limit cycle.

Two-dimensional integrator with obstacle avoidance. The dynamics \(x_{t+1}=x_t+u_t\) are linear and decoupled, so the difficulty lies entirely in a nonlinear state constraint,

$$h_{\mathrm{obs}}(x)=\|x-x^o\|^2-(p_o/2)^2\ \ge\ 0 ,$$

a circle of radius 1.5 centered at the origin. The task is to reach a terminal setpoint from initial conditions placed so that the direct path is blocked. The obstacle enters the loss through a one-sided hinge, and a terminal term evaluating the policy at the target is required for the reaching task to settle.

Baselines

SD-DPC is compared against three baselines. IPOPT is an optimization benchmark solved per scenario with exact model knowledge and the same costs, so it serves as a performance reference rather than a competitor. NN-DPC is a deep policy with two hidden layers of 32 ReLU units, trained by the same closed-loop objective on the same identified model. NN-Distilled regresses a sparse policy onto the actions of the trained NN-DPC policy, restricted to the terms SD-DPC retained, so the two differ only in how the coefficients on a common support are obtained.

Results

Identified models

On held-out trajectories, the \(N=50\) step rollout mean-squared errors of the identified models are \(7.64\times10^{-10}\), \(3.30\times10^{-8}\) and \(4.71\times10^{-7}\) for the two-tank, Van der Pol and 2-D integrator systems, with a maximum absolute state error below \(1.37\times10^{-3}\).

Sparsity selection

The retained terms depend jointly on the \(\ell_1\) weight \(\lambda_\pi\) and the pruning threshold \(\tau_\pi\). We select the sparsest policy whose performance on a separate set of validation scenarios is not degraded relative to the dense end of the sweep. The price of sparsity is system dependent: for the Van der Pol oscillator the loss is flat across the sweep, whereas for the two-tank system it degrades once \(\lambda_\pi\) is pushed beyond the selected point.

Closed-loop evaluation

All controllers are evaluated on the true plants over 500 held-out scenarios. SD-DPC satisfies the constraints in every scenario, evaluates in 17 µs per control step and occupies under 0.1 kB, roughly two orders of magnitude less memory than NN-DPC and far below the 18–26 MiB allocated by each IPOPT solve.

Learned policies

For the two-tank system, SD-DPC retains six terms across the two input channels and tracks each reference segment with a loss about twelve times lower than NN-DPC. The distilled policy on the same six terms leaves a steady offset and about seventeen times the error. The raw channel outputs, with \(u=\Pi_{[0,1]^2}(v)\) and \(r\) the level reference, are

$$\begin{aligned} v_1 &= 0.6198\,r + 1.4659\cos 2x_1 + 2.1415\cos 2x_2 - 3.4944\cos 2r,\\ v_2 &= -4.2401\cos 2x_1 + 4.2429\cos 2x_2 . \end{aligned}$$

The pump command combines a reference-dependent feedforward with feedback on both levels, while the two nearly equal valve coefficients make the valve open only when the upper tank is fuller than the lower one.

For the Van der Pol oscillator, all controllers stabilize the origin and satisfy the constraints. SD-DPC retains three terms, with a loss comparable to NN-DPC and about half that of the distilled policy:

$$u=\Pi_{[-5,5]}\big(\kappa_1x_1+\kappa_2x_2+\kappa_3x_1^2x_2\big),\qquad \kappa=(-0.9873,\ -2.8860,\ 0.2716).$$

Because the model and the policy are finite expansions over analytic libraries, the closed loop is available in closed form. For the unsaturated loop, \(V=\tfrac12(1-\kappa_1)x_1^2+\tfrac12x_2^2\) gives \(\dot V=(\mu+\kappa_2)x_2^2-(\mu-\kappa_3)x_1^2x_2^2\le0\) whenever \(\kappa_1<1\), \(\kappa_2<-\mu\) and \(\kappa_3\le\mu\), conditions satisfied by the learned coefficients, so the origin is globally asymptotically stable.

For the 2-D integrator, SD-DPC retains twelve terms, steers around the obstacle and reaches the setpoint from every sampled initial condition, with cumulative control effort within 0.6% of the IPOPT minimum. The distilled policy on the same terms violates the obstacle constraint in 60.8% of the scenarios. Its coefficients are regressed onto the teacher's actions without the obstacle penalty: the imitation error is at most 0.08 on the states visited by the teacher, where the regression data lie, but reaches 0.35 on those visited by the student, while the near-optimal teacher clears the obstacle by only 0.06, leaving no margin to absorb it.

Sensitivity bounds

Weighting each retained coefficient by the slope of its library function certifies \(L_\pi\le18.4\), \(16.0\) and \(0.32\) for the three systems, whereas the largest \(\|\partial_x\pi\|\) computed by automatic differentiation on a dense grid is \(12.0\), \(9.4\) and \(0.19\). The certificate is therefore conservative by a factor of at most 1.7, and the pruning bound held at every prune.

Code

The implementation uses Python 3.10, PyTorch 2.6 and Neuromancer 1.5.3, with CasADi for the optimization benchmark. Paper configs, publication checkpoints and tutorial notebooks are included for each case. 

git clone https://github.com/SOLARIS-JHU/SD-DPC.git && cd SD-DPC
python3.10 -m venv .venv && source .venv/bin/activate && pip install -e .

CASE=TwoTank                             # or VanDerPol, DoubleIntegrator
python scripts/$CASE/system_id.py        # sparse dynamics + debias refit
python scripts/$CASE/train_sd_dpc.py     # sparse dictionary policy
python scripts/$CASE/train_nn_dpc.py     # neural DPC baseline
python scripts/$CASE/evaluation.py       # evaluate on the true plant
