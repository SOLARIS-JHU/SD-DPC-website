Abstract

Sparse, interpretable policies, trained in closed loop

We present sparse dictionary differentiable predictive control (SD-DPC), a framework for learning sparse, interpretable feedback policies for nonlinear systems from data. A prediction model is first identified by rollout-based sparse identification of nonlinear dynamics (SINDy), building on gradient-based and multistep formulations. The policy is then parameterized as a sparse combination of dictionary functions and trained by differentiating a constrained finite-horizon predictive-control objective through this model, so that its terms are selected by closed-loop performance rather than by imitating a previously trained controller. The result is an explicit feedback law with only a handful of terms. Across three benchmark control problems, SD-DPC satisfies the constraints in all test scenarios, outperforms a policy distilled onto the same terms by up to an order of magnitude, and requires orders of magnitude less memory and online computation than an optimization benchmark, while admitting explicit sensitivity bounds.

Method

Two sparse stages, one thresholded gradient procedure

The prediction model and the feedback law are both sparse expansions over explicit dictionaries, and both are sparsified by the same sequentially thresholded gradient descent. Nothing in the pipeline imitates a pretrained controller.

STAGE 01

Rollout-based sparse identification

The dynamics are fit from multistep rollout error instead of state derivatives, so numerical differentiation never enters the objective. With a one-step horizon and an Euler step it collapses back to standard ℓ1-SINDy.

STAGE 02

Sparse dictionary policy

The policy is a dictionary expansion projected onto the admissible inputs, so input bounds hold by construction and each channel keeps its own subset of terms.

closed-loop policy objective
$$\min_{\Xi_{\pi}}\ \frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\Big(\sum_{t=1}^{N}\ell_{\mathrm{DPC}}(x_t,u_{t-1},\psi_t)+\ell_N(x_N,\psi_N)\Big)+\lambda_\pi\|\Xi_\pi\|_1$$ $$x_{t+1}=F_{\Xi}(x_t,u_t),\qquad u_t=\pi_{\Xi_\pi}(z_t)=\Pi_{\mathcal U}\big(\Theta_\pi(z_t)\,\Xi_\pi\big)$$
GRADIENTS THROUGH SATURATION

A leaky projection surrogate

The forward pass evaluates the true saturated loop; the backward pass uses a banded, leaky derivative so coefficients feeding a saturated channel are neither silenced nor credited with sensitivity the plant does not have.

FINITE TERMINATION

Pruning that provably stops

A pruned coefficient never returns, so the retained set changes at most \(pn\) times and the thresholding loop terminates within \(n_pM(pn+1)\) epochs — for any loss.

EXPLICIT SENSITIVITY

Lipschitz bounds from coefficients

\(L_\pi\) is read off the retained coefficients and the library slopes. On the benchmarks the certificate is conservative by a factor of at most 1.7 — no layer-norm products or SDP relaxations needed.

Training dynamics

Watch the policy get sparse

Closed-loop states, the control signal, and the coefficient matrix during sparse policy learning on the Van der Pol oscillator. Pruned terms stay visible with their coefficients pinned at zero — by the end, three terms carry the whole controller.

The price of sparsity is system dependent

The retained terms depend jointly on the ℓ1 weight \(\lambda_\pi\) and the pruning threshold \(\tau_\pi\). We keep the sparsest policy whose validation performance is not degraded relative to the dense end of the sweep. For the Van der Pol oscillator the loss is flat across the sweep; for the two-tank system it degrades once \(\lambda_\pi\) is pushed past the selected point.

Case studies

Three problems, three readable controllers

constrained level tracking 2 states · 2 inputs 6 retained terms (4 + 2)

A pump feeds an upper tank that drains into a lower one. The policy tracks time-varying level references while respecting level bounds, and beats the neural DPC policy by roughly 12× in tracking loss. Distilling onto the same six terms leaves a steady offset and 17× the error.

learned raw channel outputs, \(u=\Pi_{[0,1]^2}(v)\)
$$\begin{aligned} v_1 &= 0.6198\,r + 1.4659\cos 2x_1 + 2.1415\cos 2x_2 - 3.4944\cos 2r,\\ v_2 &= -4.2401\cos 2x_1 + 4.2429\cos 2x_2. \end{aligned}$$

The pump command mixes a reference feedforward with feedback on both levels; the two nearly equal valve coefficients open the valve only when the upper tank is fuller than the lower one.

limit-cycle suppression 2 states · 1 input 3 retained terms

The uncontrolled oscillator converges to an attracting limit cycle around an unstable origin. SD-DPC stabilizes the origin from initial conditions on and off the cycle, matching the neural policy's loss with three terms — and the resulting closed loop is simple enough to analyze by hand.

closed-form stability certificate
$$V=\tfrac12(1-\kappa_1)x_1^2+\tfrac12x_2^2,\qquad \dot V=(\mu+\kappa_2)x_2^2-(\mu-\kappa_3)x_1^2x_2^2\le 0$$ $$\text{for }\ \kappa_1<1,\ \ \kappa_2<-\mu,\ \ \kappa_3\le\mu \quad\text{(all satisfied by the learned }\kappa\text{)}$$

Because the model and the policy are finite expansions over analytic libraries, the unsaturated closed loop is available in closed form: the learned coefficients render the origin globally asymptotically stable.

nonlinear state constraint 2 states · 2 inputs 12 retained terms (8 + 4)

A planar integrator must reach a setpoint whose direct path is blocked by a circular obstacle. SD-DPC steers around it from every sampled initial condition, with cumulative control effort within 0.6% of the IPOPT optimum. The distilled policy on the same terms violates the obstacle in 60.8% of scenarios — which is the point of the example.

why imitation gives no safety guarantee
$$\varepsilon_\pi\ge 0.35 \ \Rightarrow\ \text{teacher margin needed } h_{\mathrm{obs}}\ge L_hL_u\varepsilon_\pi\ge 1.05, \qquad \text{actual margin } 0.06$$

The imitation error is small on the states the teacher visits, where the regression data lie, but grows on the states the student visits. A near-optimal teacher clears the obstacle by only 0.06, so no margin is left to absorb it.

Results

Constraints met, two orders of magnitude smaller

Every controller is evaluated on the true plant over 500 held-out scenarios. SD-DPC evaluates in 17 µs and stores under 0.1 kB across all three systems — versus 5 kB for the neural policy and 18–26 MiB per IPOPT solve.

Code

Reproduce any case in four commands

The repository ships paper configs, publication checkpoints and four tutorial notebooks per case, built on Neuromancer 1.5.3, PyTorch 2.6 and CasADi. 

git clone https://github.com/SOLARIS-JHU/SD-DPC.git && cd SD-DPC
python3.10 -m venv .venv && source .venv/bin/activate && pip install -e .

CASE=TwoTank                             # or VanDerPol, DoubleIntegrator
python scripts/$CASE/system_id.py        # 1. sparse dynamics + debias refit
python scripts/$CASE/train_sd_dpc.py     # 2. sparse dictionary policy
python scripts/$CASE/train_nn_dpc.py     # 3. neural DPC baseline
python scripts/$CASE/evaluation.py       # 4. evaluate on the true plant
